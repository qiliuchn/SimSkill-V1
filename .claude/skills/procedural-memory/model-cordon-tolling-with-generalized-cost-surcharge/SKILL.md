# Model Cordon Tolling with Generalized-Cost Surcharge

Models congestion pricing / cordon tolling — a synthetic per-edge cost surcharge that reroutes traffic around a priced zone without any physical closure or real slowdown — distinct from `simulate-incident-rerouting`'s physical edge closures and `compute-dynamic-user-equilibrium`'s pure travel-time assignment.

## Identifying cordon-entry edges programmatically

Don't hand-pick the edges that constitute "entering the priced zone" — derive them from the compiled network directly (e.g. via `sumolib`, checking which edges' `to` node is inside the designated zone and `from` node is outside it). This is verifiable and reproducible, unlike a guessed edge list.

## Critical mechanism finding: the automatic rerouting device may not respond to `adaptTraveltime`

**Observed in one verified test (not independently re-confirmed with a preserved diagnostic artifact — treat as a documented empirical observation motivating this skill's design, not an unconditionally established SUMO-wide fact): SUMO's automatic rerouting device (`device.rerouting`) can route purely by its own internally measured mean-speed weights, ignoring both `traci.edge.adaptTraveltime()` and `traci.vehicle.setAdaptedTraveltime()` for its own periodic rerouting decisions** — a large synthetic toll applied only via `adaptTraveltime` produced identical cordon crossings to no toll at all in this test. However, **`traci.simulation.findRoute()` does honor `adaptTraveltime()`**. The working design:

1. Equip every vehicle with the rerouting device, but set its own automatic period very large (`--device.rerouting.period 1e9`) so it stays dormant and never fights the controller.
2. Each control period, inflate cordon-entry edges' perceived travel time via `traci.edge.adaptTraveltime(edge, freeflow_tt + toll)`.
3. For each vehicle, propose a candidate route via `traci.simulation.findRoute(current_edge, destination)` (which sees the inflated weights).
4. Switch the vehicle onto the candidate via `traci.vehicle.setRoute()` **only if it's strictly cheaper** under the same generalized-cost function used to build the candidate — never force a route unconditionally.

```python
if route_cost(cand_edges) < route_cost(remaining) - EPS:
    traci.vehicle.setRoute(vid, cand_edges)
```

**Never call any real-slowdown API** (`setMaxSpeed`, `setSpeed` with a persistent low value, `setDisallowed`) on cordon edges — the toll must be perceived-cost-only; verify this by confirming cordon-entry edges' *real* (not perceived) travel time stays flat across every toll level in the raw tripinfo/edgeData output.

## The zero-toll negative control is essential and exact

**With `toll_seconds == 0`, the candidate route generated by `findRoute` is never strictly cheaper than a vehicle's existing free-flow route, so zero vehicles ever switch — the toll=0 run should reproduce the no-controller baseline exactly** (identical cordon-crossing counts, identical travel times). Verify this explicitly rather than assuming it; it's the cleanest proof the mechanism only activates when the surcharge is nonzero.

## Instrumenting the zone: E3 (entryExitDetector)

An E3 multi-entry/multi-exit area detector records vehicles entering a defined zone and their mean in-zone travel time — the correct instrument for a cordon zone (distinct from E1 point detectors and E2 lane-area detectors used elsewhere in memory). Configure entry/exit edges matching your programmatically-derived cordon boundary.

## Verified findings

On a real 6x6 grid with a central cordon zone: the zero-toll control reproduced the baseline exactly (zero route switches, identical crossing counts and travel times). As the toll rose, cordon-zone entries fell sharply and monotonically toward a floor (vehicles whose origin or destination genuinely lies inside the zone, who must cross regardless of price), perimeter/detour-edge volume rose correspondingly, and network-wide total travel time, time loss, and total distance traveled all rose monotonically — the classic congestion-pricing tradeoff of fewer zone entries purchased at the cost of longer aggregate trips.

## Gotchas

- **The automatic rerouting device may ignore `adaptTraveltime`/`setAdaptedTraveltime`** — verify this on your SUMO version before assuming a simple weight override will work; use the `findRoute`+`setRoute` workaround if it doesn't, keeping the device's own auto-reroute period large so it stays dormant.
- **Never touch real speed/capacity on tolled edges** — a toll models perceived cost only; verify real travel time stays flat across toll levels from raw output.
- **Gate every route switch on a strict cost improvement** — an unconditional forced reroute isn't a generalized-cost model and won't reproduce a genuine zero-toll negative control.
- **Derive cordon-entry edges programmatically from the compiled network**, not by hand-picking, for reproducibility.
- **E3 is the correct detector for a zone**, not E1/E2 which measure a point or lane segment.
