# Cordon Tolling and E3 Detectors

Congestion pricing (cordon tolling) can be modeled in SUMO as a purely synthetic, generalized-cost surcharge on entering a priced zone — vehicles perceive a higher cost for a route through the zone even though the zone's edges are physically just as fast and open as before. This is distinct from [[incident-rerouting-and-closures]] (physical edge closures) and [[dynamic-user-equilibrium-and-wardrop]] (pure travel-time-based equilibrium assignment).

## A mechanism observation: the automatic rerouting device may not respond to `adaptTraveltime`

**In one verified test, SUMO's automatic rerouting device (`device.rerouting`) appeared to route purely by its own internally measured mean-speed weights, ignoring both `traci.edge.adaptTraveltime()` and `traci.vehicle.setAdaptedTraveltime()` for its own periodic rerouting decisions** — a large synthetic toll applied only through `adaptTraveltime` produced identical cordon-crossing counts to a no-toll run. This specific diagnostic was not independently re-confirmed with a preserved test artifact in the episode that surfaced it, so it should be treated as a documented empirical observation that motivated a particular workaround design, not an unconditionally verified SUMO-wide limitation — worth re-testing directly if precision matters for a given SUMO version.

**However, `traci.simulation.findRoute()` was confirmed to honor `adaptTraveltime()` weights.** The resulting working technique: keep the automatic rerouting device's own period very large (effectively dormant) so it never competes with the controller, then each control period inflate cordon-entry edges' perceived travel time via `adaptTraveltime`, propose a candidate route per vehicle via `findRoute`, and switch onto it via `setRoute` **only when the candidate is strictly cheaper** under the same generalized-cost function — never an unconditional forced reroute.

## Real travel time and capacity must remain untouched

A genuine perceived-cost-only toll never calls `setMaxSpeed`, `setSpeed` with a persistent reduced value, or `setDisallowed` on the tolled edges — verified directly: cordon-entry edges' *real* travel time (from raw tripinfo/edgeData) stayed flat across every toll level in a real test, confirming the toll changed only route choice, not actual travel conditions.

## The zero-toll negative control

**With the surcharge at zero, no candidate route generated by `findRoute` is ever strictly cheaper than a vehicle's existing route, so zero vehicles switch — the toll=0 scenario should reproduce the no-controller baseline exactly.** Verified directly: identical cordon-crossing counts, identical in-zone and entry-edge travel times, and zero logged route switches at toll=0 versus 179-220 route switches at higher toll levels in a real test — the cleanest possible proof that the mechanism only activates when the surcharge is genuinely nonzero.

## E3 detectors: the correct instrument for a zone

SUMO's E3 (multi-entry/multi-exit, `entryExitDetector`) area detector measures vehicles entering and exiting a defined region and their mean in-zone travel time — the correct detector type for a cordon zone, distinct from E1 point detectors (see [[macroscopic-fundamental-diagram]]) and E2 lane-area detectors (see [[variable-speed-limits-and-e2-detectors]]).

## Verified congestion-pricing tradeoff

On a real 6x6 grid with a central cordon zone: as toll rose from zero through low to high, cordon-zone entries fell sharply and monotonically toward a floor representing vehicles whose origin or destination genuinely lies inside the zone (who must cross regardless of price); perimeter/detour-edge traffic volume rose correspondingly; and network-wide total travel time, time loss, and total distance traveled all rose monotonically — the textbook congestion-pricing signature: fewer vehicles pay to enter the priced zone, but the aggregate cost of diverting them around it is real and measurable.
